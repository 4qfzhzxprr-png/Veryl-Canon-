Peer-to-Peer Call Notes

A running note of what actually comes up on peer-to-peer calls, kept by the medical directors on rota. It is not a criterion and it is not a policy; it is the list of arguments that keep recurring and what was decided about each.

What to record

  • Who called, when, and for which member and which request
  • The clinical argument made, in the treating clinician's own words rather than summarised into ours
  • What changed as a result: overturned, upheld, or held pending named evidence
  • Anything the call revealed about the criterion itself, which is the part that gets lost

Recurring arguments

Imaging before conservative therapy. Treating clinicians say six weeks of conservative therapy is not always achievable. The criteria allow an exception for progressive neurological deficit, and the desk was not reading it out. Fixed in the letter template rather than in the criterion.

Step therapy where the preferred drug was tried elsewhere. A trial under a previous insurer counts, and the member should not have to repeat it. Evidence is the prior insurer's pharmacy record; a clinician's attestation is enough when that record cannot be obtained.

Site of care redirection late in a course of treatment. Redirection mid-course is disruptive and rarely worth the saving. Held: no redirection after the second infusion of a course, absent a safety reason.

What these notes are not

Nothing decided on a call changes a criterion. Where a call shows a criterion is wrong, it goes to the committee that owns it, with the call recorded as the evidence. Three of the entries above became criterion changes that way, and the rest did not.
