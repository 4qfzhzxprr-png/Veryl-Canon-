Prior Authorisation Desk Guide

What the authorisation desk does with a request, in the order it does it. This is a working guide written by the desk, not the authorisation policy. Where the two differ, the policy is right.

The clocks

  • A standard request is decided within fifteen calendar days of receipt.
  • An expedited request — where the standard timeframe would seriously jeopardise the member's health or their ability to regain function — is decided within seventy-two hours.
  • One extension of fourteen days is allowed, once, and only if the member is told before the original clock runs out and told what is missing.

The clock runs from receipt of the request, not from receipt of the clinical information. Waiting for records does not stop it.

Who may decide what

  1. The desk may approve a request that meets the criteria on its face.
  2. The desk may never deny. A denial is made by a clinician qualified in the relevant specialty, and by nobody else.
  3. Where the criteria are silent, the case goes to a medical director rather than being decided by analogy with a similar one.

Peer-to-peer

A treating clinician can ask to speak to the medical director before a decision is issued, and after one. Offer it in the same call in which you tell them the case is going for review; do not make them find it. Requests come in on auth-peer@example.com and are answered within one working day.

What the letter says

Every denial letter names the criterion applied, quotes the part of it that was not met, says what evidence would have met it, and gives the appeal route and its deadline. A letter that says "not medically necessary" and nothing else comes back on appeal and should.

The criteria library is the source for every quotation above.
