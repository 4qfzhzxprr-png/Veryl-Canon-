Coordination of benefits cheat sheet

Which plan pays first when a member is covered twice. Written on the desk, checked against nothing.

The order

  1. A plan with no coordination clause pays before one that has one.
  2. The plan covering the member as an employee pays before the plan covering them as a dependant.
  3. For a child covered by both parents, the parent whose birthday falls earlier in the calendar year pays first. The birthday, not the year of birth — this is the rule people get wrong.
  4. Where parents are separated, a court order naming the responsible parent outranks the birthday rule.
  5. Everything else being equal, the plan that has covered the member longer pays first.

What we pay when we are second

The difference between what the primary plan paid and what we would have paid had we been primary, never more. If the primary plan paid more than our allowance, we pay nothing and the claim still closes as paid.

Do not tell a member "the other plan should have paid that". Take the claim, decide it, and coordinate afterwards.

The official version of this is in the claims processing standard. This page exists because the official version does not have the birthday rule in a form anyone can read at speed.
